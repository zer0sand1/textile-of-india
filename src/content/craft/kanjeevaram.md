  Kanjeevaram silk weaving traces back over 400 years to the weavers of Kanchipuram, who migrated from
  Andhra Pradesh during the Vijayanagara empire. The craft flourished under royal patronage, and the sarees
  became a hallmark of Tamil Brahminical weddings and temple ceremonies.
howItsMade: >
  Three distinct shuttles are used simultaneously — two for the body and one for the border — weaving the
  saree in one piece without joints. Pure mulberry silk threads are dip-dyed, and the zari (metallic thread)
  is traditionally made of fine silver wire coated in gold.

  Temple borders, checks (kattam), stripes (rekku), peacocks, and floral vines are signature motifs. Pure
  mulberry silk and silver-gold zari are the standard materials; lighter versions use silk-cotton blends.

Kanjeevaram (also Kanchipuram silk) is a type of silk saree woven in the Kanchipuram region of Tamil Nadu, India. These sarees are known for their durability, rich colours, and contrasting borders with intricate zari work. They are traditionally worn at weddings and festive occasions across South India.
